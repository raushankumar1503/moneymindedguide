Editorial standards

Last updated: 3 September 2026

MoneyMinded is an independent, educational personal-finance publication. Our aim is to explain everyday money topics — budgeting, saving, debt, credit, and the basics of investing — in plain language that a beginner can act on. This page explains exactly how we research, write, check, review, and maintain our content, so you can judge for yourself how much to trust what you read here.

We are not a bank, a lender, a broker, or a licensed financial-advice firm, and we don't pretend to be. Everything we publish is general information for education, not personalized advice. You can read the specifics in our disclaimer.

How we research

Our guides start from widely accepted, mainstream financial principles rather than opinion or trends. When we prepare an article, we draw on reputable, publicly available sources — including government and regulatory bodies, established educational institutions, and other recognised, high-quality references — to make sure the concepts and figures we describe are accurate and current. Where a topic differs by country (for example, tax treatment, account types, or typical deposit requirements), we say so and encourage readers to check the rules that apply where they live.

How we select sources

When we cite or point readers toward further reading, we prioritise primary and authoritative sources over secondary commentary. For financial concepts, that means we prefer official government, central-bank, and regulator material (such as consumer-protection agencies and financial regulators) and reputable non-profit or educational resources. We do not treat other blogs, forums, or promotional content as authoritative, and we avoid sources that exist mainly to sell a product. We only cite sources we actually used, and we link to them so you can verify the underlying facts yourself.

How we check facts

Before an article is published, we check that the factual claims and any numbers hold up. Worked examples and tables are calculated and re-checked for arithmetic accuracy, and any illustrative rate or figure is clearly labelled as an example rather than a promise or forecast. We deliberately avoid stating specific current interest rates, product terms, or statistics as though they were fixed facts, because those change frequently and vary by provider and region. When something genuinely depends on a reader's circumstances or location, we flag it instead of guessing.

How we use AI

We may use AI tools to help with parts of the process — for example, drafting an outline, suggesting phrasing, or speeding up research. We do not treat AI output as automatically correct or publish it unchecked. Any AI-assisted draft is reviewed, fact-checked, edited, and approved by a human before it goes live, and the explanations, examples, and structure are shaped to be genuinely useful and original. In short: AI can help us write faster, but a person is always responsible for what we publish, and the editorial judgment is human.

Human review

Every guide is read and edited by Raushan Kumar, MoneyMinded's founder and editor, before publication. That review looks at accuracy, clarity, tone, and whether the article actually helps a beginner take a sensible next step. We also check that appropriate disclaimers are present, that examples are realistic and correctly calculated, and that we have not implied any guaranteed outcome.

How we keep content up to date

Personal finance changes over time, so we treat our articles as living documents. We revisit guides periodically and when we become aware that guidance, rules, or best practice have shifted. When we make a substantive update, we revise the "last reviewed" or "updated" date shown on the page so you can see how current it is.

Corrections policy

We want to get things right, and we will fix mistakes promptly. If you spot an error — a wrong number, an out-of-date fact, a broken link, or anything unclear — please tell us through our contact page and describe the issue and the page it's on. We review every report, correct genuine errors as quickly as we reasonably can, and update the page accordingly.

Advertising and affiliate disclosure

MoneyMinded is free to read and is supported by advertising, and in some cases by affiliate relationships. That means we may display ads (including through third-party networks such as Google AdSense) and may earn a commission if you choose to use certain products or services we mention, at no extra cost to you. These commercial relationships never determine our editorial opinions, what we recommend, or the order in which we present information. We label advertising as advertising and keep it separate from our editorial content. You can read more in our disclaimer and privacy policy.

Financial disclaimer

The content on MoneyMinded is general educational information only. It is not personalized financial, investment, tax, legal, or credit advice, and it does not take your individual circumstances into account. Financial products, rates, and laws vary by location and change over time. Before making an important money decision, please verify the details with authoritative sources and consider speaking with an appropriately qualified professional. We never promise guaranteed returns, savings, loan or card approval, or any specific financial outcome.

Report an error or ask a question

Transparency only works if you can reach us. To report a correction, ask about our methods, or suggest a topic, please use our contact page. If a real email address has not yet been set up for this site, the address shown there is a clearly marked placeholder — replace it with a working address before going live.
