About MoneyMinded

Independent personal-finance education since 2026

MoneyMinded started with a simple frustration: most money advice is either written for people who already understand money, or it's trying to sell you something. If you've ever closed a finance article feeling more confused than when you opened it, this site is for you.

Our goal is to explain personal finance the way a knowledgeable friend would — in plain language, with real numbers, and without the shame that so often comes attached to money conversations. Whether you're making your first budget, digging out of debt, or trying to finally build some savings, we want the next step to feel obvious.

What we cover

We focus on the everyday fundamentals that make the biggest difference for most people: budgeting, saving, paying off debt, and building credit, plus a gentle introduction to investing. We deliberately keep things practical. You won't find hot stock tips or get-rich-quick promises here — just the boring, reliable habits that actually work.

How we work

Every guide is written to be genuinely useful first. We start from mainstream, widely accepted financial principles, research reputable published sources — including government and regulatory bodies — and use realistic, clearly labelled examples rather than hype. When a topic has trade-offs, we lay out both sides so you can decide what fits your life rather than claiming there's only one "right" answer.

We also believe in being open about our process. We may use AI tools to help with parts of our work, such as drafting an outline or tightening wording, but we never publish unchecked AI output: every article is fact-checked, edited, and approved by a human before it goes live, and a person is always responsible for what we publish. We treat our guides as living documents, revisiting them when guidance changes, and we fix mistakes promptly when readers point them out. You can read the full detail — how we research, choose sources, check facts, use AI, and handle corrections — in our editorial standards.

MoneyMinded is reader-supported. To keep the site free, we display advertising (including through third-party networks such as Google AdSense) and may earn a commission if you choose to use certain products we mention, at no extra cost to you. This never changes what we write or the order in which we present things — our guidance would be worthless if it were for sale. We keep advertising clearly separate from our editorial content.

Our editorial & research process

Every guide on MoneyMinded goes through the same process before it reaches you:

  1. Topic selection. We choose everyday money questions that beginners actually ask, and frame each guide around a clear, practical task.
  2. Research using reputable sources. We start from mainstream, widely accepted financial principles and read established, publicly available sources rather than opinion or hype.
  3. Review of primary and official sources. For rules, figures, and definitions we check the relevant primary or official source — for example government agencies and financial regulators — rather than relying on second-hand summaries.
  4. Drafting and editorial review. A draft is written, then read and edited for accuracy, clarity, and balance, so trade-offs are explained rather than glossed over.
  5. Fact-checking figures and claims. Important numbers and factual claims are checked against their source before publication, and labelled as examples when they are illustrative.
  6. Updating when things change. We treat guides as living documents, revisiting them when guidance changes and correcting mistakes promptly when readers point them out.

MoneyMinded may use AI-assisted tools during drafting or editing — for example to help outline a topic or tighten wording — but published content is reviewed and edited by Raushan under this process, and a person is always responsible for what appears on the site. You can read the full detail in our editorial standards.
